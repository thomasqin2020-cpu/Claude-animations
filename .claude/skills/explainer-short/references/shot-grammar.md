# Shot grammar: turning a sentence into a visual

Use when the user's direction leaves a line uncovered. Each line of narration needs one
primary visual that demonstrates it, and the visual changes every 2 to 4 seconds.

| Narration says | Show |
| --- | --- |
| A surprising number or comparison | Scale comparison: two objects side by side, or a bar or gauge driven by the number |
| A cause | The mechanism in cutaway or cross-section, animated with cause then effect |
| "On Earth / elsewhere" vs "here" | Match cut: identical framing, different world (colour, motion, sky) |
| A process over time | Time-lapse: counter or calendar, repeated events, accumulating result |
| A person does something | The character performs it with a prop; medium shot, hands visible |
| Damage or wear | Before state, then the change fading or growing in; close-up on the part |
| Something invisible (gas, pressure, charge, heat) | Particles, glow, thermal colour, or a gauge in the caption layer |
| Geography or route | Globe or map, line drawn in real time, camera descends to the location |
| A machine | Exploded view or moving components with the camera orbiting |
| A payoff or consequence | Pull back to the widest shot of the subject with the key number on screen |

Transitions that read well: push in on a detail then cut to its micro world; follow a
falling or thrown object into the next scene; match the framing of two worlds; cut on the
word that names the next subject; orbit around an object as it transforms.

Avoid: random camera shake, slow drifts with nothing changing, a new location that the
narration does not mention, text that repeats the narration word for word.
