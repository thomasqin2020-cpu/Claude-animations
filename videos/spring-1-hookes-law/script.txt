Pull a spring a distance x, and it pulls back with a force F equals negative k x.
The minus sign means the force always points back toward equilibrium.
k is the stiffness, measured in newtons per meter.
But why is the force linear?
Near any stable equilibrium, expand the potential energy as a Taylor series.
The slope is zero there, so the first term that matters is one half k x squared.
Take negative d U d x, and Hooke's law falls right out.
That is also why it only holds for small stretches, below the elastic limit.
