After eight hours on the Moon, Apollo 12's suits were more worn than training suits used for a hundred hours.
The culprit was dust.
On Earth, wind and water tumble sand into smooth, rounded grains.
The Moon has neither.
For billions of years, meteorite impacts have smashed its rock into jagged shards and tiny glass fragments.
Every step kicks them up.
They grind through fabric, scratch visors, and jam the wrist locks.
Brushing clears the coarse grains. The fine ones cling to everything.
Two more moonwalks, and the suits could have lost pressure.
