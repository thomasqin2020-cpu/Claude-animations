How much work does it take to stretch a spring?
The force is not constant, so you cannot just multiply force by distance.
Instead, integrate k x d x from zero to x.
You get one half k x squared, the triangle under the force graph.
That work is stored as elastic potential energy.
Going from x one to x two, the spring itself does work one half k x one squared, minus one half k x two squared.
Double the stretch, and you store four times the energy.
